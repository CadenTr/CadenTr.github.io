Case study 02 / Deeds + boundary history

Independence Park boundary history.

I am reconstructing the changing boundary of Independence Park from handwritten deeds, recorded maps, right-of-way changes, adjoining parcels, and present-day records. The goal is to show which line belongs to which moment in the park’s history.

The question / One place, several footprints

Which boundary are we talking about?

01 / Acquisition

The 1904–05 assemblage

The early park came together through several conveyances rather than one clean parent tract. I am plotting the separate deed calls and their adjoiners before combining anything.

02 / Change

Roads, school, stadium, and transfers

Later public uses and right-of-way projects changed how the park was described, occupied, and mapped. Those changes need their own dated layers instead of being folded into the original line.

03 / Present

Operational and GIS footprints

A current parcel or park-maintenance line answers a different question from a historic deed boundary. I keep those footprints separate and label the source and date for each.

Public history / A changing civic landscape

Key events around the boundary.

These events provide the historical frame for the record research. They do not replace the deeds; they explain why later lines and uses may differ.

  1. 1904Charlotte begins assembling land for its first public park.The idea was advanced in March, land was conveyed in August, and the name Independence Park followed in October.
  2. 1905Early surveys and additions describe a multi-tract park.Recorded descriptions, a division map, adjoining owners, streets, stakes, and Sugar Creek form the starting evidence.
  3. 1914A playground adds a new programmed use.The public function starts evolving even while the land records retain their own history.
  4. 1929–36Armory, memorial, rose garden, and stadium projects reshape the site.Each improvement adds another reason to distinguish occupied areas from record title.
  5. 1949–50Independence Boulevard changes the park’s edge and circulation.Transportation work becomes a major boundary and right-of-way research layer.
  6. 2023The 34-acre park reopens after a major renovation.The present public park is one endpoint in a much longer chain of land, use, and mapping decisions.

Survey research / Evidence before closure

How I am working the record.

Build the deed chain

Transcribe the calls, book and page references, stated areas, grantors, grantees, and exceptions into a source matrix.

Plot each instrument alone

Hold the written bearings and distances long enough to expose gaps, overlaps, nonclosure, and conflicting assumptions.

Use the neighborhood as evidence

Compare adjoiners, historic rights-of-way, monuments, creek calls, and recorded maps before choosing how records relate.

Separate the resulting lines

Maintain distinct historical, regulatory, transferred, and current operational footprints, each with its own source and confidence note.

Why I am not forcing a single acreage.

The early conveyances total 46.67 stated acres, while historical summaries describe roughly 47.5 acres and the present park is described as 34 acres. Those numbers represent different evidence, dates, and footprints. The difference is a research result to explain, not a gap to hide by forcing closure.
